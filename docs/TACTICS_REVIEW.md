# Kingnamic tactics and replayability review

5 October 2026. Builds on the original Broken Standard milestone. This review uses actual Chromium controls, inspected screenshots, deterministic combat tests and explicitly labeled save fixtures. No external human feedback or physical-phone performance is claimed.

## Findings and changes

The old standard was away from meaningful high ground, the two ambushes repeated one composition, and the equipment cost did not scale with recruitment. The mission now offers a high ridge and a narrow ford with marshy flanks. Three disclosed enemy mixes rotate deterministically with seed and attempt; multi-front arrivals are staggered after warnings. Enemy health and damage were not globally inflated. Capture pauses while infected contest the standard and cannot finish ahead of scheduled enemies. Retreat cancels future arrivals while living enemies continue to fight.

Wardens hold a protected front; spearmen have longer reach and bonus damage against runners, including while advancing. Rangers benefit from elevated ground and loose formation but lose 40% damage at close range. Scouts move quickly and use a shorter, faster bow with bounded evasion and low durability. Runners prefer exposed ranged soldiers when infantry cannot already intercept them. Rally fronts orient toward the closest threat, or toward the expedition's advance. Allied follow positions reserve distinct slots and prioritize catching up when separated. Formation controls remain available during expeditions; spacing changes on the next rally order, and the interface says so.

Original cached character poses now show sword follow-through, directional weapon swings, spear thrusts, arrow flight and impact sparks. Damage recoil and critical-wound markers clarify danger. Mission and territory labels fade over actors, alongside the existing terrain/building fades. No bitmap assets or dependencies were added. Dense opposing traffic still uses soft separation, so a crossing can briefly look crowded; this is not claimed to be fully solved.

New departures cost 30 provisions and four herbs plus eight provisions and eight timber reserved per potential recruit. Equipment for soldiers who do not enlist is refunded once; the travel pack is spent. A fresh four-member battalion therefore reserves 62 food, 32 timber and four herbs in total. The four-member roster remains finite. Stranded survivors retain wounds between attempts, deaths stay recorded, and returned soldiers keep the existing 20–80-second recovery system. New retry cooldown is 20 peaceful kingdom seconds, aligned with minimum troop rest rather than an extra 45-second wait. Existing active expeditions retain their original location, costs and cooldown terms.

The first-time objective card now opens the relevant building/job/army action. A browser test confirmed that replacing this button on each display update made it unreliable to click; stable cached markup fixes it. Closing a guide immediately restores the prior pause state. Extraction explains unmet conditions and waits for every survivor. A separate terminal-state regression caught trusted allies being eligible for recruitment after the entire patrol died; defeat now always awards zero recruits and refunds unused equipment.

Individual harvesting remains an aggregate economy. Existing builders already contribute one worker to one site and share finite crew effort. Adding per-frame harvest transactions would add complexity without a confirmed gameplay benefit. Civilian paths and workplace visits remain presentation routines; the economy has not been rewritten or falsely described as individual harvesting.

## Verification record

Final simulation regression: **108 tests passed across six files**, including all previous 79 and 29 additional tactical, balance and casualty cases. The 18-run balance sweep exercised both approaches, three enemy mixes and mixed, all-bow and late-relief patrol policies. All completed; some compositions lost patrol or allied members. Across the sweep, missions lasted approximately 53–73 simulated seconds. These are fixed automated policies, not proof of a human difficulty curve or broad balance.

| Automated policy | Runs | Allies recruited (of 24 possible) | Total patrol + ally casualties |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Mixed infantry/bow | 6 | 22 | 2 |
| All bows | 6 | 21 | 4 |
| Mixed with delayed relief | 6 | 22 | 2 |

The all-bow penalty produces a practical risk but does not make that patrol unusable. Delayed relief does not show a convincing disadvantage in this sweep; supply timing needs human evaluation before stronger tuning. All 18 successes should not be presented as proof of challenging difficulty.

All **four new tactics browser checks passed** in a serial suite: guided opening through first night; a complete ford expedition with formation changes, combat reload and permanent recruitment; an explicitly reconstructed previous active-save shape at its original standard and cost; and a terminal-state fixture where real brute attacks killed the patrol, awarded zero recruits and allowed army rebuilding after reload. The successful ford run returned three patrol soldiers and recruited four allies with no casualties; all seven had persistent recovery timers. The new-player guidance proxy built two workplaces, assigned miners and a healer, recruited two soldiers and defeated the first nine infected with the Hearth undamaged and zero JavaScript errors.

The final ford combat sample measured **8.5 ms median / 17.7 ms 95th-percentile frame interval** over 240 frames, with 12 peak units, three peak effects and approximately 35.2 MiB JavaScript heap. The route completed in 65.4 simulated seconds. These are sampled browser intervals including scheduling/rendering, not isolated simulation costs or a guaranteed frame rate on other devices. Earlier concurrent runs produced a 58.3 ms p95; load conditions differ, so this is not claimed as a controlled performance improvement.

TypeScript checking and the final production build passed. Production files total **1,363,732 bytes raw / 384,869 bytes individually gzipped**, 3,040 gzip bytes (0.80%) above the original battalion milestone. No new dependencies or downloaded assets were added. The existing Vite large-chunk advisory remains.

All **11 original kingdom browser regressions passed** in their final serial run: desktop construction/jobs/recruitment/rally/upgrade/pause/save; live night combat, plague treatment and territory claims; phone layout/reset; keyboard/camera/modal behavior; blocked storage; daytime timing; resize; finite shared construction crews and saved progress; individual civilian continuity and an older v2 fixture; pointer-anchored zoom; and actual touch placement with invalid-ground rejection and 44 px controls. The **full five-night campaign passed** through actual UI controls, without injected resources, skipped simulation time or a prepared save. It finished at 552 simulated seconds / 306 wall seconds: all four territories held, 108 infected defeated, two total losses, 15 cures, eight new buildings, seven soldiers and 1,500 Hearth health. There were zero JavaScript errors. The final changes afterward only corrected route-specific expedition log wording; all 108 simulation tests and TypeScript/build checks were rerun afterward, followed by a ford browser retest.

The development inspector is absent from the production build. The final offline suite took 39 seconds. In total this milestone verified **23 distinct browser scenarios** (11 kingdom, four expedition, four tactics, three offline, one full campaign); the additional ford retest is not double-counted.

## Save and delivery behavior

The schema remains version 2. New approach, composition, reserved equipment, scheduled-arrival and stranded-wound fields are optional and bounded. An old active expedition stays at its original standard and receives no retroactive fee; its health, trust and soldiers continue normally. Both old and new mission saves are exercised separately from uninjected playthroughs. Recruitment/extraction consume the active mission once, and unused equipment refunds are part of that same transition.

Review of the existing preview also confirmed an older cached build could remain active while its page stayed open. The generated worker now precaches before activating immediately and uses network-first navigation with an offline fallback. A controller change offers save/reload guidance without forcing a running game to reload. The existing day-three preview kingdom was saved, paused, released from its old worker and reopened on the new interface with its resources and army retained. The delivered local preview remains paused on the expedition briefing at port 4186.

## Remaining priorities

1. Observe first-time human players through a complete chapter and expedition. Test whether route previews, formation changes, contested capture and recovery requirements are understood without coaching. Automated guidance is a proxy only.
2. Tune tactical pressure from human evidence. The automated late-relief policy was often similar to prompt relief; the current supply-timing choice may be too forgiving. Tower-heavy kingdom play also remains strong. The finite, equipment-funded recruits reduce reward inflation but do not establish perfect progression balance.
3. Improve directional poses and congestion at narrow crossings, then test opposing civilian/army traffic on physical low-end phones. Current touch checks emulate Chromium; Safari, Firefox and physical Android remain unverified.
4. Consider save export/import and longer campaign migration fixtures before expanding content. One authored mission with two approaches and three enemy mixes remains the scope; there is no endless recruitment campaign.
