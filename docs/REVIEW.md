# Kingnamic gameplay and polish review

4 October 2026. Existing first playable improved in place; no deployment, push, new dependencies, external art or account integration.

## Findings and resulting changes

| Area | Review finding | Improvement and practical limit |
|---|---|---|
| Village life | Workers followed synchronized straight lines through buildings; changing assignments changed identities; nightfall teleported everyone home. | Residents retain individual positions and identities, use weighted paths around structures and water, visit workplaces, carry supplies, work at construction sites, stroll and rest. They walk to shelter before dusk and seek home/refuge when ill or threatened. Normal chapter populations each have an actor; larger populations use at most 48 representatives. |
| Animation | Simulation steps looked abrupt; soldiers walked while paused; every occupation looked similar. | Smoothed character presentation, simulation-time walk cycles, tool/work and attack poses, distinct work clothing and cargo. Pausing freezes movement, smoke and effect timing. These remain simple procedural silhouettes, with mirrored rather than fully directional poses. |
| Graphics | The village was static, supporting labels were tiny, and construction/damage were understated. | Chimney smoke, warmer night pools, lighter vignette, scaffolding, damaged-building tint, hit flashes and clearer resource/job text. No bitmap assets added. Terrain trees still use a single cached background and lack independent character occlusion. Building levels remain small badges rather than alternate architecture. |
| Camera | Wheel zoom drifted away from the inspected ground; camera keys could affect the map behind a modal. | Pointer-anchored wheel zoom and modal/focused-control guards. Drag and home/minimap controls retained. Touch zoom buttons enlarged; pinch zoom remains absent. |
| Combat clarity | Full-length arrow lines and weak impact marks made attacks hard to follow. Melee could strike through occupied footprints. | Short moving arrow trails, attack poses, impact bursts, victim/building flashes and obstacle checks for melee. Arrow travel is visual: damage still resolves immediately in the existing fixed-step combat rules. Ranged fire continues to use the game's abstract range model. |
| Troops | Rally commands lost to aggro; blocked formation slots overlapped; rangers chased beyond their protective line. | Explicit move orders take priority, destinations reserve distinct slots, rangers hold ground and wardens have bounded pursuit. An actual campaign then exposed passive guards at a breach; idle guards now return to defend the Hearth. Retreat orders still take priority. No new squad-selection feature was introduced. |
| Building interactions | Immediate touch spending made placement mistakes expensive; construction sites offered little visual feedback. A cleansing button implied an action was available before its refuge existed. | Real touch input now previews a site, marks invalid ground and requires Build here before spending. Mouse placement retains its familiar single click. Scaffolding clarifies construction. Cleansing availability now checks its building and supplies. Roof selection and existing inspection/repair/upgrade flows are preserved. |
| Pacing and infection | An unguided opening with five soldiers survived several nights, but by day four sickness had reduced the workforce to nine ill residents. A single alert hid other threats. | Alerts combine attackers and sickness, show the nearest turning deadline and offer a direct route to treatment. Direct browser interaction recovered all nine sick residents. The economy, wave timings and nine-minute chapter duration were not retuned. Recovered residents still need new job assignments. |
| Difficulty | The first polish campaign lost just after the fifth dawn despite seven surviving soldiers. | Corrected breach defense rather than changing enemy health or granting resources. The repeated UI campaign won with the same general legal strategy. This validates that strategy, not broad novice difficulty: its full-health Hearth finish suggests further human difficulty review is warranted. |
| Phone usability | Small job, camera and footer buttons; cramped three-column build cards. | Two-column building cards, readable supporting text, 44-pixel tested touch controls, smaller objective obstruction, safe preview/confirmation, and scrollable management. Verified with actual touch events in Chromium at 390×844, not on a physical phone. |
| Enjoyment | The core build/defend/treat/expand loop worked, but village activity and command feedback made it feel detached. | Work trips, varied timing, shelter movement, clearer threats and responsive orders connect that existing loop to the visible world. Combat is more readable, though sound and character animation remain basic and no external player-enjoyment study was conducted. |

## Verification performed

- **43 passing simulation/regression tests** on the final source. Existing deterministic simulation, campaign, navigation, economics, infection, persistence and bounded-stress tests retained. Eleven new checks cover stable civilian identities/assignments, obstacle-free routes and pause, gate/river passage, shelter without teleporting, construction/refuge destinations, rerouting/actor bounds, legacy saves, reserved rally positions, retreat priority, ranger ground holding, breach defense and melee obstruction (some checks exercise multiple behaviors).
- **All nine browser checks passed** after the breach correction. Real buttons/canvas clicks exercised construction, job assignment, recruitment, rally, formations, upgrade, pause, save/reload, natural night combat, first-dawn infection, quarantine/treatment, territory claims, camera controls, dialogs, blocked storage and real touch preview/confirmation. Pointer anchoring was within two world pixels in the camera assertion. Phone controls tested at least 44×44 pixels and no horizontal overflow.
- The subsequent renderer cleanup clears sprites and damage-history caches on a new kingdom, preventing stale identity/tint data from following a reset. The affected phone placement/reset case passed again; strict production build and production offline save/reload passed again. No combat/economy changes followed the successful campaign.
- **Complete live UI campaign won:** 552.5 simulation seconds, 356 wall seconds including normal command pauses at 2×; five nights, all three marches, 108 defeated infected, 17 cures, one casualty, 30 villagers, seven soldiers and 1500 Hearth health. Zero JavaScript errors. Actions used ordinary buttons and canvas clicks; no resource injection or time jumps. [Result](evidence/campaign-polish.json) · [Victory](evidence/campaign-polish.png).
- The failed pre-correction campaign is preserved: lost at 557.7 simulation seconds, 98 kills, four casualties and seven soldiers remaining. [Failure data](evidence/polish-campaign-before-fix.json) · [Screenshot](evidence/polish-campaign-before-fix.png). This failure drove the breach-response change.
- **Production offline verification passed** after the final rebuild: service worker cached the game, network was disabled, the game reloaded, saved units resumed, and the UI remained usable. Zero external requests and zero JavaScript errors; production excludes the development inspector. Tests used port 4187, preserving the ordinary 4186 preview.
- **Save compatibility:** the save key and schema remain v2. A fixture captured from the pre-polish build decodes without changing resources, population, jobs, buildings, units or progress. Browser tests reload existing-format saves and new pending troop orders, and compare buildings, units, jobs and resources. The optional move-order value is validated; old saves need no such field. Existing v1 migration, malformed-save rejection and backup recovery tests remain passing. Civilian routes are presentation state, reconstructed on reload; no economy RNG or saved IDs are consumed by them.

One touch test was interrupted by a development reload while code was being edited, not treated as a pass. It passed in the dedicated stable-source rerun and in the subsequent complete nine-case run. The initial CUA review used an isolated localhost origin, while automated playtests used fresh browser contexts; the user's existing kingdom was not reset.

## Performance and delivery

At 1440×960, an early-settlement Chromium WebGL sample recorded **16.7 ms median**, **41.8 ms p95**, **49 engine FPS** and **29 MiB JS heap**, with campaign/build work running concurrently. An earlier sample recorded 16.6/33.2 ms, 58 FPS and 30 MiB. These are short host measurements, not claims of sustained battle FPS, GPU memory or low-end Android performance.

Final production output: **1,327,022 bytes raw**, **372,781 bytes gzip** across all shipped files. Relative to the milestone record, that is about **0.8% more raw bytes** and **0.9% more compressed bytes**. [File measurements](evidence/polish-build-size.json). Procedural art remains cached and no downloaded assets are shipped. Civilian pathfinding is capped at two searches per visual update with retries for unreachable goals; existing building/unit/effect caps remain intact. Vite's existing large-main-chunk advisory remains, with a successful build.

Play locally at http://127.0.0.1:4186/ (production/offline preview) or http://127.0.0.1:5173/ (development). Saves are separate per origin/port. Existing project architecture and uncommitted first-milestone work remain in place.

## Remaining priorities

1. New-player difficulty and infection comprehension: the verified strategy succeeds decisively, while unattended illness still punishes missed treatment. Human playtests should guide tuning rather than another speculative balance change.
2. Physical mobile, Safari/Firefox, longer battle/heap/battery measurements, pinch zoom, and keyboard-only canvas placement. Desktop touch emulation is not device certification.
3. Terrain/object occlusion, less crowd overlap at doorsteps, deeper direction/attack animations, stronger enemy silhouettes, and richer but lightweight sound. Civilians navigate individually but production remains aggregate; they do not physically determine gathered-resource delivery or catch infections from their presentation coordinates.
4. UI state explanations: some disabled job/recruit/territory actions still rely on nearby capacity/cost text or command feedback. Wall/gate inspection still needs map selection; there is no army subgroup selection. Cross-device saving/export and multi-tab save conflict handling remain outside this polish pass.
